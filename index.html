Motivation and Overview

Evaluating the social reasoning abilities that support collaboration in naturalistic multi-party meetings.

Effective collaboration requires more than understanding what people explicitly say. In meetings and other professional interactions, people continuously infer others’ beliefs, intentions, knowledge, attitudes, and unspoken disagreement to coordinate, negotiate, and adapt their communication. These latent social states are distributed across verbal and non-verbal cues, and apparent agreement can mask private dissent, creating pseudo-consensus that is difficult to detect from surface behavior alone. While such Theory-of-Mind (ToM) reasoning is fundamental to human collaboration, current multimodal AI systems are still largely evaluated on overt and externally verifiable signals.

MeetingToM addresses this gap by evaluating multimodal large language models in naturalistic four-person meetings through a hierarchical benchmark spanning subject-, dyadic-, and group-level reasoning. Built on the AMI Meeting Corpus, it contains 1,800 annotated clips from 60 meetings, covering individual mental-state inference, addressee and interpersonal-attitude reasoning, and group consensus and hidden dissent. By jointly examining verbal and visual evidence, MeetingToM tests whether current MLLMs can move beyond surface-level recognition toward the socially grounded reasoning required for real-world collaboration.
